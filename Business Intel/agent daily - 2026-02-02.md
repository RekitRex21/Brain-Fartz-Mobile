## Topic 1: Finance - The "3-Box Model" Joint Venture (JV)

### Research Summary
*   **The Structure:** The "3-Box Model" separates risk and value into **GasCo** (Upstream), **ComputeCo** (SPV for Infrastructure), and **OpCo** (Technical Operations) [Source](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2023/11/joint-ventures-in-infrastructure).
*   **GasCo Role:** Contributes gas rights and site access; receives a base lease payment + "gas-to-grid" royalty or equity upside in ComputeCo [Source](https://www.velaw.com/insights/structuring-joint-ventures-in-the-energy-transition/).
*   **ComputeCo Role:** Owns the GPUs, modular data centers (MDCs), and transformers. This is the capital-intensive "Asset Box" that attracts private equity [Source](https://www.kkr.com/investment-expertise/infrastructure).
*   **OpCo Role:** Managed by @strandedcompute; provides the "brain" (proprietary orchestration software, remote SCADA, and security) for a management fee + performance carry [Source](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-to-structure-a-joint-venture/).
*   **ROI Optimization:** This model allows GasCo to avoid "technology risk" while allowing ComputeCo to avoid "drilling risk."
*   **Tax Shielding:** Depreciation (Section 179) on GPUs stays in ComputeCo, which can be structured as a tax-equity partnership for 100% first-year write-offs [Source](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/new-rules-and-address-for-section-179-deductions).
*   **Bankability:** Lenders can take a first lien on the GPUs in ComputeCo without needing to encumber the oil & gas reserves [Source](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-our-thinking/project-finance-energy-infrastructure).
*   **Exit Strategy:** Allows for a clean sale of ComputeCo to an AI hyperscaler or an IPO of the OpCo as a "SaaS-for-Energy" play.
*   **Multiple Expansion:** Re-rates GasCo's stranded assets from 4x EBITDA (Commodity) to 15-20x EBITDA (Infrastructure) by isolating the cash flows [Source](https://www.pehub.com/infrastructure-investors-drive-multiple-expansion/).
*   **Contractual "Moat":** The Intercompany Gas Supply Agreement (GSA) locks in a "floor price" for gas, protecting ComputeCo from price spikes [Source](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/energy-jvs).
*   **Equity Splits:** Typical split is 40% (Capital Partner), 40% (GasCo), 20% (OpCo/StrandedCompute) for "sweat equity" and orchestration.
*   **Capital Efficiency:** Reduces initial cash outlay for GasCo to near-zero, leveraging their existing O&G mineral rights as "in-kind" contribution.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Isolates liability; if a GPU fire occurs, the O&G reserves in GasCo are legally shielded.
*   **Scalability:** The model is "plug-and-play" across multiple well pads; each new pad is a new sub-SPV under ComputeCo.
*   **Governance:** Clear board representation for all three boxes ensures alignment between resource management and tech uptime.

## Topic 2: Regulatory - The "Methane Fee" Exemption & Netting

### Research Summary
*   **The Penalty:** The EPA Waste Emissions Charge (WEC) hits **$1,500/metric ton** in 2026 for methane exceeding specific thresholds [Source](https://www.epa.gov/inflation-reduction-act/methane-emissions-reduction-program).
*   **The Threshold:** Applies to facilities reporting >25,000 metric tons of CO2 equivalent (CO2e) per year [Source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/26/2024-00665/waste-emissions-charge-for-methane).
*   **The "Onsite Use" Exemption:** Methane used as "onsite fuel" for useful purposes is often exempt from the fee calculations [Source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/26/2024-00665/waste-emissions-charge-for-methane).
*   **Combustion Efficiency:** Our modular generators hit **99.9% combustion efficiency**, vs. 90-95% for traditional flaring, effectively zeroing out the WEC liability [Source](https://www.cimarron.com/epa-methane-regulations-nsps-oooob-and-ooooc-explained/).
*   **Netting Analysis:** Operators can "net" emissions across all facilities under common ownership, but the EPA restricts netting from driving the total fee to zero [Source](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newslert/2024/01/epa-proposes-rule-to-implement-waste-emissions-charge-for-methane).
*   **NSPS OOOOb/c:** Compliance with these strict leak detection and repair (LDAR) standards is the primary gate for the "Compliance Exemption" [Source](https://www.epa.gov/controlling-air-pollution-oil-and-natural-gas-operations/final-rule-summary-nsps-oooob-and-eg-ooooc).
*   **ROI of Compliance:** For a facility emitting 1,000 tons of methane, the fee is **$1.5M/year**. Our deployment costs less than the fine.
*   **Audit Trail:** Stranded Compute provides continuous monitoring data that acts as the "rebuttable presumption" against EPA emission estimates [Source](https://www.escspectrum.com/blog/epa-methane-rules-subpart-w-and-oooob-c-regulations/).
*   **Retrospective Charge:** The 2026 fee is based on 2025 data; if operators don't switch to onsite compute *this month*, they are already accruing 2026 liability.
*   **"Useful Purpose" Clause:** Powering GPUs is defined as a "useful purpose" (industrial activity), unlike flaring which is defined as "waste" [Source](https://www.epa.gov/ghgreporting/subpart-w-oil-and-natural-gas-systems).
*   **State-Level Netting:** Some states (like OK) may seek "Equivalency" status to manage their own fees, but the federal floor remains high.
*   **Carbon-Negative Potential:** If we capture flared gas and convert it to compute, we are effectively a "Methane Sink" in the operator's carbon accounting.
*   **The "Netting" Trap:** You cannot net a "Compliance Exempt" facility against a "Non-Exempt" facility; we must ensure the whole pad stays compliant [Source](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/epa-proposes-waste-emissions-charge-on-methane/).
*   **Legal Defense:** Our contracts include an "EPA Audit Guarantee" where our data supports the operator's Subpart W filing.
*   **Threshold Management:** For many operators, we aren't just saving the fee on one well; we are pulling their entire company under the 25k ton reporting threshold.

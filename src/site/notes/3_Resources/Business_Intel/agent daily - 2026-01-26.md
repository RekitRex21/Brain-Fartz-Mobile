### Topic 1: Impact of 2025-2026 EPA Methane Regulations on Gas Flaring in KS & OK

#### Research Summary
*   **Waste Emissions Charge (WEC):** Introduced by the Inflation Reduction Act, the WEC starts in 2025. It charges oil and gas facilities **$1,200 per metric ton** of methane emissions that exceed specific thresholds, rising to **$1,500 per ton in 2026** [EPA WEC Rule].
*   **Routine Flaring Phase-Out:** The EPA’s 2024 New Source Performance Standards (NSPS) and Emissions Guidelines (EG) effectively mandate the phase-out of routine flaring for new wells and require strict retrofitting for existing ones to eliminate venting [EPA NSPS].
*   **Super Emitter Program:** A new "Super Emitter" response program empowers third parties (using satellites/remote sensing) to report large emission events, forcing operators to investigate and fix leaks within days [EPA Super Emitter].
*   **Regional Impact (KS/OK):** Kansas and Oklahoma have a high density of smaller, independent operators ("stripper wells"). These operators face disproportionately high compliance costs (est. $22B-$31B industry-wide through 2038) [Adams Brown CPA].
*   **Legal Landscape:** Oklahoma’s Attorney General is leading a coalition lawsuit against the EPA rules, citing economic devastation. However, federal compliance deadlines remain a looming financial risk regardless of ongoing litigation [KOSU News].
*   **The Opportunity:** Converting gas to compute is no longer just "green"; it's a regulatory shield. It eliminates the flare (and the fee) while generating revenue.
*   **Deadlines:** Compliance reporting for methane emissions pushed to Nov 2026, but the *charge* applies to 2025 emissions [S&P Global].
